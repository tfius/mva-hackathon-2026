The rule counts the disease's defining genotype as evidence against it. Drop BP1 → Likely pathogenic. Written up as a methods point.

Pretrained checkpoint + trained GraphMask gates came down from the TxGNN Explorer bundle, so no CPU training needed. Two env fixes on the way: pandas 2 removed `DataFrame.append` (pinned 1.5.3), and PyTorch 1.10's executable-stack flag blocks loading on kernel 7.0.

**A correction that changes the answer.** Ranking TxGNN's raw 7,957 drug scores nominates *Casimiroin*, *Dithioerythritol* and a run of unnamed PDB fragments — all degree-2 nodes the model knows nothing about, and on a disease with no drug edges it scores exactly those highest (ρ = −0.51 against degree). TxGNN's own `Ranked List` is the 1,801 drugs with real indication/contraindication edges; within that set the artefact vanishes (ρ = +0.054). Would have produced a confident, meaningless answer.

**Two convergences, both predicted before the run.** Hydroxychloroquine rank 50 and chloroquine 51 of 1,801 (2.8th percentile) — chloroquine is one of Amon's three aneuploidy-selective compounds, reached here from a completely different direction. Dasatinib rank 69 (3.8%) — the approved half of the senolytic pair whose founding experiment used the BubR1 mouse.

**H1 is invisible to the graph.** Nicotinamide riboside, NMN and NADH carry no indication edges in PrimeKG at all — they are supplements. The best-supported, most allele-specific hypothesis in the report cannot be evaluated by any KG-only pipeline. Coverage finding, not a refutation.

**My contraindication prediction failed, informatively.** Paclitaxel ranks 7 of 1,801 on *indications* (0.4%), vinblastine 17, eribulin 19. The graph knows MVA is a cancer-predisposition syndrome and retrieves sarcoma chemotherapy; it does not know the checkpoint is already hypomorphic, because that fact lives in the variant, not the disease node. Mechanism right, model wrong — and it is the most clinically consequential line in Track 2.

**TxGNN Explain, and a claim I had to walk back.** Raw GraphMask paths for MVA are pure hub artefact: every top path for every drug ran `MVA ← Colon cancer → Pimecrolimus ← CYP3A4 → <drug>`, scoring 0.5159 / 0.5152 / 0.5123 / 0.5074 for four unrelated drugs. Drug-independence is the tell. Excluded side-effect edges, down-weighted by degree, dropped 658 ADME hubs. **Then I over-claimed** — reported "zero non-hub paths for every candidate but paclitaxel", which was my beam width (3,000), not the graph. At 12,000 every candidate has paths. Corrected in the report; the surviving paths are thin but real (dasatinib via EPHA2/KIT into Bloom syndrome — another CIN cancer-predisposition syndrome).

**Structural reason, countable.** BUB1B has 464 edges in PrimeKG and zero drug edges. Same for BUB1, BUB3, CEP57, TRIP13, CDC20, MAD2L1 — every gene the MVA node touches. `MVA → BUB1B → drug` cannot exist. Meanwhile AURKB, PLK1, TTK and CENPE *are* druggable — exactly the proteins you must not inhibit here. One AURKB ligand in PrimeKG is **reversine**, used in the lab to *induce* aneuploidy.

**The near miss.** BUB1B's 95 interactors include **SIRT2, CREBBP and EP300**. PrimeKG encodes the whole acetylation axis H1 rests on — it just has no drug edge from the NAD+ precursors, because they are supplements. The graph holds every link but the last, and TxGNN cannot surface the best hypothesis in the report while sitting one edge from it.

**So changed method.** `05_mechanism_anchored.py` walks outward from the MVA genes instead of ranking 1,801 drugs: 526 interactors, 592 reachable drugs, each with the interactor it came through. Contraindication flag fires correctly on fostamatinib, enzastaurin, wortmannin (via AURKB/PLK1). HDAC inhibitors reachable via BUB1B–HDAC1/2/3/4 — logged as an open question, not a recommendation, since SIRT2 is class III and not inhibited by them.
